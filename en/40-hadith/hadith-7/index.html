The Religion Is Sincere Advice

What nasihah means, and to whom it is owed

A simple explanation of the hadith “The religion is nasihah (sincere advice)”, the seventh hadith in Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith. What does nasihah really mean? Learn what sincerity to Allah, His Book, His Messenger, the leaders of the Muslims and ordinary Muslims means, how to deal with a scholar's mistake, and the etiquette of giving advice: sincerity, privacy, gentleness and encouragement.

The hadith

عَنْ أَبِي رُقَيَّةَ تَمِيمِ بْنِ أَوْسٍ الدَّارِيِّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: "الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ." قُلْنَا: لِمَنْ؟ قَالَ: "لِلَّهِ، وَلِكِتَابِهِ، وَلِرَسُولِهِ، وَلِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَعَامَّتِهِمْ."

The Prophet (ﷺ) said, “The deen (religion) is naseehah (advice, sincerity).” We said, “To whom?” He (ﷺ) said, “To Allah, His Book, His Messenger, and to the leaders of the Muslims and their common folk.”

Reported by Muslim — an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, no. 7

The hadith & its narrator

Assalamu alaikum, I'm Heba. If this is your first time here: I'm summarizing Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, one hadith at a time, using mind maps and simple explanations, for anyone who doesn't have time for long lessons. And remember: what cannot be done completely should not be left completely. So let's begin: hadith number seven.

If someone told you: the whole religion is in one word, what would you expect it to be? Prayer? Fasting? The Prophet, peace be upon him, said: The religion is nasihah, sincere advice! So what does nasihah mean? And sincere advice to whom? That's today's hadith.

عَنْ أَبِي رُقَيَّةَ تَمِيمِ بْنِ أَوْسٍ الدَّارِيِّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: "الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ." قُلْنَا: لِمَنْ؟ قَالَ: "لِلَّهِ، وَلِكِتَابِهِ، وَلِرَسُولِهِ، وَلِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَعَامَّتِهِمْ

From Abu Ruqayyah Tamim ibn Aws ad-Dari, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Prophet, peace be upon him, said: The religion is nasihah, sincere advice. We said: To whom? He said: To Allah, to His Book, to His Messenger, and to the leaders of the Muslims and their common people.

The narrator is Tamim ibn Aws ad-Dari, may Allah be pleased with him. The name ad-Dari comes from one of his ancestors, called ad-Dar ibn Hani. And his kunya, his 'father of' name, is Abu Ruqayyah, after his daughter, his only child.

And this teaches us that it's perfectly normal to be called after your daughter; the companions did that. And some were called after something else entirely, like Abu Hurayrah, which means 'father of the little cat', because he always had a small cat with him.

Some scholars said this hadith is a quarter of the religion, and some said the whole religion is in it. And once you understand what nasihah means, you'll really feel that the whole religion is inside it.

And notice the Prophet's way of teaching, peace be upon him: he said a short, general phrase, the religion is nasihah, and then he paused. So the companions paid attention and asked: To whom? Just like he once asked: Do you know who the questioner was? A general statement makes you ask, and asking makes the information stick.

What is nasihah?

So what does nasihah mean? The famous meaning is guiding someone to good. But notice a small detail in Arabic. There's a difference between nasahahu, which means he guided him to good, and nasaha lahu, which means he was sincere toward him. And the companions didn't ask 'who?', they asked 'to whom?', so they understood it in the wider meaning.

The root of the word nasihah means purity and filtering. Arabs say nasahtu al-asal: I filtered the honey, meaning I cleaned it of impurities. And from it comes tawbah nasuh, sincere repentance: a repentance that's pure, for Allah, and complete.

So nasihah means giving someone their right purely, and in full. Purity: you give them their right clean, with nothing mixed in. Fullness: you give them their right completely. So nasihah is a right given in full, and pure.

Nasihah to whom?

So nasihah to Allah means giving Allah His right, pure and complete. You believe in His oneness, and free yourself from shirk. You purify your worship from showing off and self-admiration. You don't direct any supplication, sacrifice, or vow to anyone but Him. And He is the one you love most in your heart, and you love what He loves.

And nasihah to the Messenger, peace be upon him: you believe in his message, you believe what he told us, you follow what he commanded, you stay away from what he forbade, and you defend him and his Sunnah.

And nasihah to the Book of Allah: you believe it is the word of Allah, not created, and that Allah has protected it from being changed. You believe what it tells us, you follow its commands, and you stay away from what it forbids.

The leaders of the Muslims are of two kinds: those in authority, and the scholars. In the lesson, it's explained that a ruler is only called a Muslim authority, wali al-amr, if he rules by the Book of Allah. And nasihah to him means you want good for him and pray for him, because when he is upright, the whole nation becomes upright. You advise him politely if he makes a mistake. And you obey him in what is right, and there is no obedience in disobeying Allah.

And nasihah to the scholars: you love them, you help them, you spread their knowledge, and you serve them, even if it's just giving the shaykh a ride in your car. And you defend them if they're treated unjustly.

And if you hear an accusation about a scholar, the lesson says to deal with it in steps. First, make sure: did he really say that? And did he really mean it that way? Just as we check that evidence is authentic, and what it means. If not, defend him. And if he really did say it, look: does it fall under acceptable disagreement? Then there's no reason to attack him.

And if he really made a clear mistake? Don't insult him right away; he has a long record of good. Go talk to him politely, ask him, and discuss it with evidence. And if he insists, don't follow him in the mistake, and explain his error politely, without smearing him.

And nasihah to the common Muslims is a wide door: you give them their right of support and love, and you help them obey Allah. And part of it is nasihah in its famous meaning: guiding them to good, even if it goes against your own interest.

In the lesson, a story is told about the companion Jarir ibn Abdullah, may Allah be pleased with him. A man came to buy a horse from him, and offered him a large sum. But Jarir kept lowering the price, until it reached what he felt the horse was really worth. When they asked him why, he said: I pledged to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, to be sincere to every Muslim.

When I looked it up, I found that this pledge is authentically reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim: Jarir said he pledged to the Prophet, peace be upon him, to establish prayer, to give zakat, and to be sincere to every Muslim. As for the horse story, the well-known version, reported by at-Tabarani, is the other way around: Jarir was the buyer, and he kept raising the price for the seller, until he reached what the horse was really worth. Either way, the meaning is the same: sincere advice, even against your own interest.

Another example from the lesson: a doctor with a patient who doesn't need medicine, or any lab tests. Nasihah is telling him: you don't need them, even if the doctor has a deal with a company or a lab. And if someone asks your advice about a business partner or a marriage, advise them honestly.

Manners of advice

And finally, the manners of giving advice. First: sincerity. You advise because you love the person, not to shame them, or to show you're better than them. Second: in private, as much as you can. Third: hinting instead of naming names, the way the Prophet, peace be upon him, used to say: What is the matter with some people...

And fourth: gentleness and kindness. Allah said to Musa and Harun, Moses and Aaron, peace be upon them both, as they were going to Pharaoh:

فَقُولَا لَهُ قَوْلًا لَيِّنًا لَعَلَّهُ يَتَذَكَّرُ أَوْ يَخْشَى

So speak to him with gentle words, so that perhaps he may remember, or fear.

And fifth: start with praise and encouragement. Tell him the good things about him, and that you love him for the sake of Allah. Then offer him the advice as if it's wrapped in a little gift box, and he'll accept it right away. And after the advice, encourage him: Come on, let's do it together.

Review & practice

Let's review: nasihah means giving the right, pure and complete. To Allah: tawhid and sincerity. To His Messenger: believing and following. To His Book: faith and action. To the leaders of the Muslims: praying for them, and polite advice. And to the common Muslims: love, and honest advice.

Your practice this week: if you're going to advise someone, do it in private, start with what's good in them, and make your intention that you love them. And pray for good for those in authority, and for the scholars.
