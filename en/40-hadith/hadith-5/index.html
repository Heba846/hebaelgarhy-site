Whoever Innovates in Our Matter, It Is Rejected

Bid'ah and the condition of following the Sunnah

A simple explanation of the hadith “Whoever introduces into this matter of ours what is not from it, it is rejected”, the fifth hadith in Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith. Learn what bid'ah (innovation in religion) means, whether a “good bid'ah” exists, the meaning of “what a good innovation” and “whoever starts a good practice”, worship vs. everyday life, and why following the Sunnah is a condition for a deed to be accepted.

The hadith

عَنْ أُمِّ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أُمِّ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَائِشَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهَا، قَالَتْ: قَالَ: رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم "مَنْ أَحْدَثَ فِي أَمْرِنَا هَذَا مَا لَيْسَ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ رَدٌّ وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ لِمُسْلِمٍ: مَنْ عَمِلَ عَمَلًا لَيْسَ عَلَيْهِ أَمْرُنَا فَهُوَ رَدٌّ".

The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) said, “He who innovates something in this matter of ours (i.e., Islam) that is not of it will have it rejected (by Allah).” In another version in Muslim it reads: “He who does an act which we have not commanded, will have it rejected (by Allah).”

Reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim — an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, no. 5

The hadith & its narrator

Assalamu alaikum, I'm Heba. If this is your first time here: I'm summarizing Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, one hadith at a time, using mind maps and simple explanations, for anyone who doesn't have time for long lessons. And remember: what cannot be done completely should not be left completely. So let's begin: hadith number five.

Imagine someone doing an act of worship with a really good intention, putting so much effort into it, and on the Day of Judgment they find it rejected! How? That's what the fifth hadith will tell us: just one line, but it opens a big chapter of the religion.

Scholars explain that an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith isn't an easy series, as many people think. Imam an-Nawawi collected the hadiths that Islam revolves around, and they hold the general principles of the religion. If you understand them well, books like Riyad as-Salihin and Sahih Muslim will be much easier for you later.

The narrator of this hadith is the Mother of the Believers, Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her. Her kunya, an Arabic honorific name, was Umm Abdullah, the mother of Abdullah, after her nephew Abdullah ibn az-Zubayr, and because Abdullah is one of the names most beloved to Allah. After Abu Hurayrah, she's one of the companions who narrated the most from the Prophet, peace be upon him.

عَنْ أُمِّ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أُمِّ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَائِشَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهَا، قَالَتْ: قَالَ: رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم "مَنْ أَحْدَثَ فِي أَمْرِنَا هَذَا مَا لَيْسَ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ رَدٌّ

From the Mother of the Believers, Umm Abdullah Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: Whoever introduces into this matter of ours something that is not part of it, it will be rejected.

وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ لِمُسْلِمٍ: مَنْ عَمِلَ عَمَلًا لَيْسَ عَلَيْهِ أَمْرُنَا فَهُوَ رَدٌّ

And in a narration of Muslim: Whoever does a deed that is not in accordance with our matter, it will be rejected.

The condition of following

Imam Ahmad said that Islam revolves around three hadiths. First: Actions are only by intentions, which is the condition of sincerity. Second: today's hadith, which is the condition of following the Prophet. And third: The halal is clear and the haram is clear, which covers the details of the law.

So for any act of worship to be accepted, it needs two conditions: sincerity to Allah, and following the Prophet, peace be upon him. And Allah brought both together in one verse:

فَمَنْ كَانَ يَرْجُو لِقَاءَ رَبِّهِ فَلْيَعْمَلْ عَمَلًا صَالِحًا وَلَا يُشْرِكْ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِ أَحَدًا

So whoever hopes to meet his Lord, let him do righteous deeds, and not associate anyone in the worship of his Lord.

This hadith has two narrations: Whoever introduces into this matter of ours something not part of it, it is rejected. And: Whoever does a deed not in accordance with our matter, it is rejected. The second is broader: it includes the one who invented the innovation, and also the one who imitates him in it.

What is bid'ah?

So what is bid'ah? In the Arabic language, anything new is called bid'ah: the mobile phone is a bid'ah, the car is a bid'ah. But in religious terms, the word has a special meaning related to the religion, just like salah, the prayer: in the language it means supplication, and in religious terms it's specific movements at specific times.

The religious meaning in one sentence: anything new in the religion, done to get closer to Allah, that isn't part of it. Imam ash-Shatibi defined it as: an invented path in the religion, resembling the legislated one, followed in order to go to extremes in worshipping Allah.

And the words “in the religion” solve a big problem. When someone tells you: but the phone is a bid'ah, and the car is a bid'ah! Tell them: those are worldly matters. The Prophet, peace be upon him, himself used the catapult at Khaybar, and Umar set up government registers. The bid'ah we're talking about is only in worship.

And the words “resembling the legislated one” mean it looks like correct worship, and that's what causes confusion. Like someone who sets aside the 27th of Rajab for fasting, believing it has a special virtue. When you tell them that's a bid'ah, they say: so fasting is forbidden now?

And that's the secret of why bid'ah is so dangerous. The sinner knows he's wrong, and doesn't attribute his sin to the religion, so he repents easily. But the person of bid'ah thinks he's doing good, and attributes it to the religion, so it's hard for him to repent. And if you advise him, he says: may Allah guide you!

Worship vs. dealings

And here's an important rule: in acts of worship, the default is that they're not allowed, except what Allah has legislated. In dealings, food and drink, the default is that they're allowed, except what Allah has forbidden. So in worship: only do what you've been told. And in food and trade: do what you like, except what you've been told is forbidden. Allah says:

أَمْ لَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ شَرَعُوا لَهُمْ مِنَ الدِّينِ مَا لَمْ يَأْذَنْ بِهِ اللَّهُ

Or do they have partners who have legislated for them in religion what Allah has not permitted?

Here's a nice example from the lesson: if someone asks you: what's your evidence that zucchini is halal? You'll tell them: the default in food is that it's allowed, so you bring me evidence that it's forbidden. But if someone comes up with a new act of worship, he's the one who has to bring the evidence, not you.

Doubts & replies

Some people use the hadith: whoever starts a good practice in Islam, to argue that we can invent nice new things in the religion. But the story behind the hadith explains its meaning. Some poor people came to the Prophet, peace be upon him, and he asked for charity. No one got up, until a man brought a heavy bag, and people followed his lead. So “started” here means he revived an existing Sunnah, not that he invented something.

Like the first person who revived sitting in the mosque until sunrise, or praying Eid in the open, or someone who set up a prayer space at work, and people started praying again. These people started a good practice: they revived it, they didn't invent it.

So is there such a thing as a good bid'ah in religion? No. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said: every bid'ah is misguidance. And the word “every” is one of the strongest words of generality. Besides, who decides that it's good? I see it as good, and you see it as bad! That's why legislation must come from Allah. And Allah says:

الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِي وَرَضِيتُ لَكُمُ الْإِسْلَامَ دِينًا

This day I have perfected for you your religion, and completed My favor upon you, and approved for you Islam as your religion.

Imam Malik said: whatever was not religion on that day is not religion today. And he said that whoever claims there's a good bid'ah in Islam is as if claiming that the Prophet, peace be upon him, didn't deliver the message completely.

But didn't Umar, when he gathered people for the Taraweeh night prayer in Ramadan, say: what a good bid'ah this is? The Prophet, peace be upon him, prayed Taraweeh in congregation for three nights, then stopped, because he feared it would become obligatory for us. When the Prophet passed away, that fear was gone, so Umar brought it back. So it's a Sunnah, and “bid'ah” here is in its language meaning.

And this gives us an important rule: any act of worship that the Prophet, peace be upon him, didn't do, even though the reason for it existed and nothing prevented him, is a bid'ah. But if there was something preventing it, or the reason didn't exist yet, that's a different matter.

Like Abu Bakr compiling the Quran: during the Prophet's lifetime, peace be upon him, revelation was still coming down, and that was an obstacle. When revelation ended, and many Quran reciters were killed in the Riddah wars, the wars against apostasy, the reason grew stronger and the obstacle was gone, so it was compiled. And Uthman's first call to prayer for Friday: the reason appeared when Medina grew and people lived farther away.

Types of bid'ah

There are two important types of bid'ah. An original bid'ah, with no basis in the religion at all, like the mawlid celebrations. And an added bid'ah: the basis is legitimate, but something is added to it, like group dhikr, remembrance of Allah, in one voice. The dhikr itself is worship, but that way of doing it is an addition.

In a report that al-Albani graded authentic: Abu Musa al-Ash'ari found people sitting in circles in the mosque. One man would tell them: say Allahu akbar a hundred times, and they would count with pebbles. So Ibn Mas'ud went to them and said: you have introduced something new into Allah's religion. They said: we only wanted good. He said: and how many who want good never reach it!

The narrator says he later saw most of those people fighting alongside the Khawarij, an extremist rebel group, on the day of Nahrawan. So bid'ah starts small and grows, and a good intention doesn't fix a wrong deed.

Bid'ahs come in other types too. An action bid'ah, like doing a new act of worship. And an abstaining bid'ah, like forbidding yourself something halal as an act of worship, like the man who said: I will never marry. And there are bid'ahs in belief, and bid'ahs in practice.

And not everyone who commits a bid'ah is a disbeliever. If his bid'ah clashes with something known in the religion by necessity, something every Muslim knows, it can take him out of Islam. If it's less than that, he's a sinful Muslim. And for some bid'ahs, scholars themselves differ on the ruling.

And there are things scholars differ on, whether they're Sunnah or bid'ah, because they differ on whether the hadith about them is authentic, like Salat at-Tasbih, the prayer of glorification, and the virtue of the night of mid-Sha'ban. Here, follow the scholar you trust, and don't condemn those who differ with you.

And one last doubt: but you give a lesson every Friday, that's specifying a time! The rule: what the law has restricted, like saying subhanAllah thirty-three times after prayer, stays exactly as it is. And what the law left open, like sending blessings on the Prophet, peace be upon him, you can organize: a hundred times every day, for example. That's organizing, not legislating.

But if you start telling people: you must do these exact numbers, they have a special virtue, and if you add or take away, it won't work, then you've turned organizing into legislating, and that's a bid'ah.

Its harms & our duty

The harms of bid'ah are many. Every bid'ah that rises kills a Sunnah in its place. Its follower is as if saying the religion is incomplete. It's hard for him to repent. It makes people lose interest in the Sunnah. And it grows over time.

So what's our duty? Not just replying to bid'ahs: we must spread the Sunnah and keep people busy with it, because if you don't keep yourself busy with the truth, it will keep you busy with falsehood. And if you see a bid'ah, advise gently and with knowledge.

Review & practice

Let's review: any act of worship needs sincerity and following the Sunnah. Bid'ah is something new in the religion, done to get closer to Allah. The default in worship is that it's not allowed, and in dealings that it's allowed. There's no good bid'ah, and “starting a good practice” means reviving one.

Your practice this week: before any act of worship, ask yourself: did the Prophet, peace be upon him, do it this way? Pick a forgotten Sunnah and revive it. And if you see something wrong, advise kindly.
