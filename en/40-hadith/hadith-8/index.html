8

I Have Been Commanded to Fight the People

Jihad and the protection of life and wealth

A calm, simple explanation of the hadith “I have been commanded to fight the people…”, the eighth hadith in Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith. What is jihad and what is its goal? Did Islam spread by the sword? Learn the difference between embracing Islam by free choice and political rule, “there is no compulsion in religion”, jizyah, when a person's life and property are protected, and “their reckoning is with Allah”.

The hadith

عَنْ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم قَالَ: "أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أُقَاتِلَ النَّاسَ حَتَّى يَشْهَدُوا أَنْ لَا إلَهَ إلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَيُقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ، وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ؛ فَإِذَا فَعَلُوا ذَلِكَ عَصَمُوا مِنِّي دِمَاءَهُمْ وَأَمْوَالَهُمْ إلَّا بِحَقِّ الْإِسْلَامِ، وَحِسَابُهُمْ عَلَى اللَّهِ تَعَالَى" .

The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) said, "I have been ordered to fight against the people until they testify that there is none worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, and until they establish the salah and pay the zakat. And if they do that then they will have gained protection from me for their lives and property, unless in Islam, and their reckoning will be with Allah."

Reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim — an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, no. 8

The hadith & its narrator

Assalamu alaikum, I'm Heba. If this is your first time here: I'm summarizing Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, one hadith at a time, using mind maps and simple explanations, for anyone who doesn't have time for long lessons. And remember: what cannot be done completely should not be left completely. So let's begin: hadith number eight.

Many people ask: did Islam spread by the sword? What is jihad, really? And what is its goal? Our eighth hadith answers these questions, and the lesson explains it point by point. So let's go through them one at a time.

All of an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith are examples of jawami' al-kalim: few words, with a huge meaning. That's why the lesson explains each hadith as a series of points, so we can understand it piece by piece.

عَنْ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم قَالَ: "أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أُقَاتِلَ النَّاسَ حَتَّى يَشْهَدُوا أَنْ لَا إلَهَ إلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَيُقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ، وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ

From Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: I have been commanded to fight the people until they testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, and establish the prayer, and pay the zakat.

فَإِذَا فَعَلُوا ذَلِكَ عَصَمُوا مِنِّي دِمَاءَهُمْ وَأَمْوَالَهُمْ إلَّا بِحَقِّ الْإِسْلَامِ، وَحِسَابُهُمْ عَلَى اللَّهِ تَعَالَى

If they do that, they have protected their lives and their wealth from me, except by the right of Islam, and their reckoning is with Allah, the Exalted.

This hadith is narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both. And it's agreed upon, meaning it's in both al-Bukhari and Muslim.

What is jihad?

Point one: what does jihad mean? In the lesson, it's defined as giving one's wealth and oneself, so that guidance reaches people without distortion, and they can choose it without fear or threat. And the Prophet, peace be upon him, called it the highest peak of Islam.

And the standards here come from revelation, not from people. We can't measure jihad by human standards that keep changing. And the words “I have been commanded”, at the start of the hadith, settle the matter.

The lesson explains that the greatest human right is a person's right to know Allah and enter Paradise. Guidance matters even more than food. In a hadith qudsi, where the Prophet reports Allah's words, Allah says, meaning: O My servants, all of you are astray except those I guide. And only after that: all of you are hungry except those I feed.

That's why Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, described this nation as: the best of people for people. And the Prophet, peace be upon him, said, meaning: Allah marvels at people who enter Paradise in chains. These are people who entered Islam after the conquests, and became people of Paradise.

But doesn't Allah say, meaning: there is no compulsion in religion? Yes. And the lesson explains: no one is forced to enter Islam, because someone who enters it against their will is a hypocrite, and forcing anyone is of no use. Jihad removes the barriers that stop the message from reaching people, and the choice remains theirs.

Its types

Point two: the types of jihad. Defensive jihad: when someone attacks Muslim lands. It's an obligation on everyone who is able, without needing permission. And initiated jihad, called jihad at-talab: Muslims going out with the call to Islam to other lands. Scholars explain that this is established, and it's a communal obligation, fard kifayah, meaning if some fulfill it, the rest are excused.

And this initiated jihad, a communal obligation, becomes a personal obligation, fard ayn, in two cases. First: when the two armies meet in battle. Allah says, meaning: do not turn your backs to them. And second: when the leader assigns a specific person, like a specialist, a pilot for example.

The lesson gives examples: Yemen and Medina entered Islam without fighting. The conquest of Egypt involved limited fighting, and afterwards millions embraced Islam over time. Compare that with the Crusades and the World Wars, in which millions died.

Spread by the sword?

So, did Islam spread by the sword? The lesson separates two things. Embracing the faith, people actually becoming Muslim: no, that's by free consent. Whoever doesn't accept it pays the jizyah, a tax paid by non-Muslim citizens, and keeps their own religion. But governance: yes. The sword had a role in opening lands, so that Islam would govern them, and the message could reach people.

“I have been commanded”

Point three is in the words “I have been commanded”: the Prophet, peace be upon him, acted by the command of revelation, which is free from error, not by his own opinion. Allah says, meaning: Today I have perfected your religion for you. And this is a great blessing: Allah spared us confusion, because human laws keep changing through trial and error.

Even some Jews said to Salman al-Farisi: your Prophet has taught you everything. Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, used to pray: O Allah, give us a clear ruling on wine, waiting for revelation to decide. And Umm Ayman cried after the Prophet, peace be upon him, passed away, and said she was crying because revelation from the heavens had stopped.

When does fighting end?

Point four: when does fighting end? The word “until” in the hadith marks the end point, meaning up to when, not the reason why. And here's a question: the hadith says until they testify, but the verse in Surat at-Tawbah says, meaning: until they pay the jizyah. So how do they fit together?

The explanation: the hadith mentions the furthest goal, which is Islam, and the verse mentions a nearer goal, which is the jizyah. The hadith of Buraydah makes it clear: the Prophet, peace be upon him, instructed his army commanders to offer three things, in order: Islam; if they refuse, the jizyah; and if they refuse, fighting. And as the lesson puts it: we come to save people, not to kill them.

So who is the jizyah taken from? The Prophet, peace be upon him, took it from the Magians, the Zoroastrians. And scholars differed. The Malikis said: from everyone, and this is the view preferred in the lesson. The Hanafis said: from everyone except the Arab idol-worshippers. And the Shafi'is and Hanbalis said: only from Jews, Christians and Magians.

Protection

The next point: when are a person's life and wealth protected? The basis of this protection is the two testimonies of faith, the shahadah. In the story of Usamah ibn Zayd, may Allah be pleased with them both, he killed a man in battle after the man said: there is no god but Allah. Usamah said the man only said it out of fear of the weapon. So the Prophet, peace be upon him, said to him: Did you split open his heart?

And this protection continues as long as the person stays firm on the foundation of Islam, and commits to its rulings. That's the meaning of “except by the right of Islam”. So if someone does something that carries a ruling, or a fixed legal punishment, called a hadd, in Islamic law, it is applied to them.

The lesson gives examples, as scholars explain them. A person who abandons prayer is asked to repent. Adultery carries a fixed legal punishment: flogging or stoning. And whoever refuses to pay zakat, it is taken from them by the authorities, and some scholars allowed an added financial penalty. And of course, these rulings are applied by the courts and the legitimate authorities, not by individuals.

Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, fought those who refused to pay zakat, and he said: By Allah, if they withheld from me even a camel's hobbling rope that they used to give to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, I would fight them for withholding it. And the lesson explains that they were an organized group refusing by force, meaning rebels, not apostates, people who had left Islam.

The same applies to any group that collectively refuses a public rite of Islam. But note: the rules for fighting rebels are different from the rules for fighting disbelievers. With rebels, no captives are taken, there are no spoils of war, and the wounded are not finished off.

Their reckoning is with Allah

And the end of the hadith: “and their reckoning is with Allah”. That means we judge by what's outward, and Allah takes care of what's hidden in hearts. The Prophet, peace be upon him, treated the hypocrites as Muslims, and even prayed the funeral prayer over Ibn Salul, until Allah forbade him. And the lesson explains that outwardly, people are either Muslim or non-Muslim, and whoever openly shows disbelief after being Muslim is an apostate.

Review & practice

Let's review: jihad is striving so that guidance reaches people without compulsion, and it has two types: defensive and initiated. Islam entered hearts by free consent. Protection comes through the shahadah, and continues by committing to its rulings. And we judge by the outward, while their reckoning is with Allah.

Your practice this week: know your religion well, so if someone tells you Islam spread by the sword, you can answer with knowledge. Don't judge people's intentions; we only judge what's outward. And ask Allah to keep you firm on Islam.
