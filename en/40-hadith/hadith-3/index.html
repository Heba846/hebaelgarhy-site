Islam Is Built on Five

The five pillars of Islam

A simple explanation of the hadith “Islam is built on five”, the third hadith in Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith. Learn the five pillars: the testimony of faith, prayer, zakat, fasting and Hajj, why the Prophet (peace be upon him) described Islam as a building, and what scholars say about abandoning prayer. Includes Arabic recitation and a translation of the meaning.

The hadith

عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: سَمِعْت رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم يَقُولُ: " بُنِيَ الْإِسْلَامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ: شَهَادَةِ أَنْ لَا إلَهَ إلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَإِقَامِ الصَّلَاةِ، وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ، وَحَجِّ الْبَيْتِ، وَصَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ". .

I heard the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) say, "Islam has been built on five : testifying that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the salah (prayer), paying the zakat (obligatory charity), making the hajj (pilgrimage) to the House, and fasting in Ramadhan."

Reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim — an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, no. 3

The hadith & its narrator

Assalamu alaikum, I'm Heba. If this is your first time here: I'm summarizing Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, one hadith at a time, using mind maps and simple explanations, for anyone who doesn't have time for long lessons. And remember: what cannot be done completely should not be left completely. So let's begin: hadith number three.

Imagine a building that's only columns, with no walls and no finishing. Could anyone live in it? Of course not. But without those columns, there's no building at all. And that's exactly what the third hadith teaches us.

This hadith is narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father. And here's a nice tip to remember the narrators: the first two hadiths are from Umar ibn al-Khattab, the third is from his son, Abdullah ibn Umar, and the fourth is from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud.

Imam an-Nawawi did us a favor: he removed the long chains of narrators and left us only the companion's name. So the least we can do is memorize the companion's name. And this hadith is reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim, so it's at the highest level of authenticity.

عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: سَمِعْت رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم يَقُولُ:

From Abu Abd ar-Rahman, Abdullah, the son of Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, say:

بُنِيَ الْإِسْلَامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ: شَهَادَةِ أَنْ لَا إلَهَ إلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَإِقَامِ الصَّلَاةِ، وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ، وَحَجِّ الْبَيْتِ، وَصَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ

Islam is built on five: testifying that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the prayer, giving the zakat, making the pilgrimage to the House, and fasting Ramadan.

“Built”: the five columns

These pillars were already mentioned in the hadith of Jibril. But what's new here is the word: built. It means these five are the foundations that all of Islam stands on: the shahada, prayer, zakat, Hajj, and fasting.

So the hadith of Jibril defined Islam for us, and today's hadith tells us that these five are the foundation. Take them away, and there's no building at all. That's why Imam an-Nawawi named his book: The Forty, on the Foundations of Islam and the Principles of its Rulings.

And notice that Hajj comes before fasting here, while in other narrations, fasting comes before Hajj. This doesn't affect the meaning. Either the Prophet, peace be upon him, said it more than once, or the order comes from the narrator.

Columns, walls & finishing

But be careful: these pillars are the columns, not the whole house. You also need walls, and those are the obligations and avoiding what's forbidden, like honoring your parents and keeping family ties. And then comes the finishing: the recommended acts. And everyone finishes their house as much as they strive.

And no one should say: Islam is built on five, so I'll do just these five. No! There are many other obligations, like honoring your parents and keeping family ties, and avoiding what's forbidden, like adultery and drinking alcohol. These aren't pillars, but they're the walls of the house, and they must be built.

Imagine two apartments in the same building, with the same columns and the same walls. One has just bare walls, and it passes. The other has luxury finishing, and it sells for many times the price. That's us: the minimum is the pillars and the obligations, and after that, everyone has their own finishing.

In another hadith, a man asked the Prophet, peace be upon him, about the obligations, and said: By Allah, I won't add to them, and I won't take anything away. The Prophet said: He will succeed, if he is truthful. So whoever does the pillars and the obligations will succeed, God willing; that's the apartment that passes. But whoever wants higher ranks finishes it with the recommended acts.

Why these four?

So why these four in particular? Prayer is worship with the body. Zakat is giving from your money, which your soul loves. Fasting is holding yourself back from things you love. And Hajj brings all of that together. And with that, our servitude to Allah becomes complete.

Let's go into a bit more detail. Zakat is called: giving what you love, because money is dear to the soul, and you give it for Allah. Fasting is the opposite: leaving what you love. You leave the food and drink your soul wants. So one is about giving, and the other is about holding back.

As for Hajj, it brings all of that together: you pay your money, you tire your body, and you leave your country, your family and your work for days. That's why Allah made it obligatory only once in a lifetime, and only for those who are able.

Some people are active in one thing and slack in another. One says: anything with my body, I'm in, but not money! Another pays easily, but fasting is hard for him. And these four together cover all of that.

What if a column falls?

So if one of these columns falls, does the whole house fall? The first column, the shahada, is agreed upon: whoever never said it, or said it and then broke it, the whole house has collapsed. Like Abu Talib: he knew it was true, but he refused to say it.

And the shahada only counts if it comes from the heart and the tongue. A person must believe it, accept it, and submit to it, and say it. Iblis, the devil, knew there is no god but Allah, and Pharaoh was certain of it, but both refused out of arrogance. And Abu Talib refused because of what people would say. The result is the same.

The Prophet, peace be upon him, kept saying to his uncle Abu Talib as he was dying: Uncle, say: there is no god but Allah, a word I can plead for you with before Allah. Abu Talib loved him and defended him all his life, but he refused to say it, and he died that way.

And whoever enters Islam must also protect it, because some things nullify it: like mocking the prayer, the hijab, or the Prophet, peace be upon him, or denying something that is clearly known to be part of the religion, like saying: prayer isn't obligatory.

An example of what we hear these days: some people say that prayer in the Quran only means supplication, du'a. This idea leads a person to deny the five daily prayers that all Muslims know, and that is a very serious danger.

As for the other four pillars: whoever leaves them while denying that they're obligatory has left Islam, by agreement. But whoever leaves them out of laziness or stinginess, while still accepting that they're obligatory, there is a difference of opinion. The majority, Malik, ash-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa, say he's not a disbeliever. And in Imam Ahmad's school, there are narrations that the one who abandons prayer is a disbeliever. The disagreement is strongest about prayer.

And this disagreement comes in degrees: it's strongest about prayer, then zakat, then fasting, and weakest about Hajj. And even within Imam Ahmad's school, there are many views, including one that agrees with the majority: that whoever leaves prayer out of laziness is not a disbeliever.

So why is the disagreement about leaving prayer so strong? Because each side has strong evidence. Those who consider it disbelief cite the hadith in Sahih Muslim: Between a man and shirk, associating partners with Allah, and disbelief, is abandoning the prayer. And the hadith in at-Tirmidhi: The covenant between us and them is the prayer, so whoever abandons it has disbelieved. And the majority answered with strong evidence too, first of all, the words of Allah:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَنْ يُشْرَكَ بِهِ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ

Indeed, Allah does not forgive that partners be associated with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills.

So leaving prayer out of laziness, as long as it isn't shirk or denial, falls under: what is less than that. And there's also the hadith: Five prayers Allah has made obligatory on His servants, reported by Abu Dawud and graded authentic by al-Albani. It says that whoever neglects them, his matter is with Allah: if He wills, He punishes him, and if He wills, He forgives him. And a disbeliever is not included in that will.

And about zakat too: the Prophet, peace be upon him, mentioned the owner of cattle who doesn't pay their zakat. His cattle will trample him on the Day of Judgment, on a day as long as fifty thousand years, and then he will see his path, either to Paradise or to the Fire. So there's still a chance of Paradise, which means he died a Muslim.

The lesson leans toward the majority view, but with an important warning: the disagreement here isn't between a disbeliever and a good person. It's between a disbeliever and a fasiq, a sinner committing a major sin. So leaving prayer is a disaster either way, and we must never take it lightly.

Priorities in practice & teaching

This hadith also teaches us priorities. If you wake up late and only five minutes are left, pray the obligatory prayer before the Sunnah prayer. If your money is limited, zakat comes before voluntary charity. If it's either a new car or Hajj, then Hajj comes first. And Isha, the obligatory night prayer, is more important than all of Taraweeh, the extra night prayers of Ramadan.

Sadly, in Ramadan some people care a lot about Taraweeh, and rush through Isha to catch it. No: Isha is obligatory, and the obligatory comes before all the voluntary prayers. Focus on the obligatory first, pray it with humility, and then add more.

It's the same idea when calling people to Islam. Imagine a young man who just started coming to the mosque, wearing a chain necklace, or with a tattoo. Where do you start with him? Start with belief and prayer, and the other things later, one step at a time, so it isn't too heavy on him.

When the Prophet, peace be upon him, sent Mu'adh to Yemen, he told him: the first thing to call them to is tawhid, the oneness of Allah. If they understand that, teach them the five daily prayers. If they understand that, teach them about zakat. Exactly the same order as our hadith. And this hadith is in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

And it's the same order when teaching people: we start with belief, then prayer, then zakat, just as the Prophet, peace be upon him, advised Mu'adh when he sent him to Yemen. But priorities don't mean neglecting the rest, and Allah says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا ادْخُلُوا فِي السِّلْمِ كَافَّةً

O you who believe, enter into Islam completely.

Review & practice

Let's review together: Islam is built on five columns, and the shahada is its foundation. Columns alone aren't a house: you need the obligations, and the recommended acts are the finishing. The four acts of worship together complete our servitude to Allah. And the hadith teaches us to start with what matters most.

Your practice this week: pray every obligatory prayer on time, before any voluntary prayer. If you owe zakat, pay it before giving voluntary charity. And pick one thing from the finishing to add to your house.
