The Hadith of Jibril

Islam, iman and ihsan

A simple explanation of the famous Hadith of Jibril, the second hadith in Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith. Learn the difference between Islam, iman (faith) and ihsan (excellence), the five pillars of Islam, the six pillars of faith, belief in divine decree, and the signs of the Hour. The hadith is recited in Arabic, followed by a translation of its meaning.

The hadith

عَنْ عُمَرَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَيْضًا قَالَ: " بَيْنَمَا نَحْنُ جُلُوسٌ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم ذَاتَ يَوْمٍ، إذْ طَلَعَ عَلَيْنَا رَجُلٌ شَدِيدُ بَيَاضِ الثِّيَابِ، شَدِيدُ سَوَادِ الشَّعْرِ، لَا يُرَى عَلَيْهِ أَثَرُ السَّفَرِ، وَلَا يَعْرِفُهُ مِنَّا أَحَدٌ. حَتَّى جَلَسَ إلَى النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه و سلم . فَأَسْنَدَ رُكْبَتَيْهِ إلَى رُكْبَتَيْهِ، وَوَضَعَ كَفَّيْهِ عَلَى فَخِذَيْهِ، وَقَالَ: يَا مُحَمَّدُ أَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ الْإِسْلَامِ. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم الْإِسْلَامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لَا إلَهَ إلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ الْبَيْتَ إنْ اسْتَطَعْت إلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا. قَالَ: صَدَقْت . فَعَجِبْنَا لَهُ يَسْأَلُهُ وَيُصَدِّقُهُ! قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ الْإِيمَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ بِاَللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ، وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالْقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ. قَالَ: صَدَقْت. قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ الْإِحْسَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّك تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاك. قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ السَّاعَةِ. قَالَ: مَا الْمَسْئُولُ عَنْهَا بِأَعْلَمَ مِنْ السَّائِلِ. قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ أَمَارَاتِهَا؟ قَالَ: أَنْ تَلِدَ الْأَمَةُ رَبَّتَهَا، وَأَنْ تَرَى الْحُفَاةَ الْعُرَاةَ الْعَالَةَ رِعَاءَ الشَّاءِ يَتَطَاوَلُونَ فِي الْبُنْيَانِ. ثُمَّ انْطَلَقَ، فَلَبِثْتُ مَلِيًّا، ثُمَّ قَالَ: يَا عُمَرُ أَتَدْرِي مَنْ السَّائِلُ؟. قُلْتُ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: فَإِنَّهُ جِبْرِيلُ أَتَاكُمْ يُعَلِّمُكُمْ دِينَكُمْ ". .

While we were one day sitting with the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) there appeared before us a man dressed in extremely white clothes and with very black hair. No traces of journeying were visible on him, and none of us knew him. He sat down close by the Prophet (ﷺ) rested his knees against the knees of the Prophet (ﷺ) and placed his palms over his thighs, and said: "O Muhammad! Inform me about Islam." The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) replied: "Islam is that you should testify that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is His Messenger (ﷺ), that you should perform salah (ritual prayer), pay the zakah, fast during Ramadan, and perform Hajj (pilgrimage) to the House (the Ka`bah at Makkah), if you can find a way to it (or find the means for making the journey to it)." He said: "You have spoken the truth." We were astonished at his thus questioning him (ﷺ) and then telling him that he was right, but he went on to say, "Inform me about Iman (faith)." He (the Prophet) answered, "It is that you believe in Allah and His angels and His Books and His Messengers and in the Last Day, and in fate (qadar), both in its good and in its evil aspects." He said, "You have spoken the truth." Then he (the man) said, "Inform me about Ihsan." He (the Prophet) answered, "It is that you should serve Allah as though you could see Him, for though you cannot see Him yet He sees you." He said, "Inform me about the Hour." He (the Prophet) said, "About that the one questioned knows no more than the questioner." So he said, "Well, inform me about its signs." He said, "They are that the slave-girl will give birth to her mistress and that you will see the barefooted ones, the naked, the destitute, the herdsmen of the sheep (competing with each other) in raising lofty buildings." Thereupon the man went off. I waited a while, and then he (the Prophet) said, "O `Umar, do you know who that questioner was?" I replied, "Allah and His Messenger know better." He said, "That was Jibril. He came to teach you your religion."

Reported by Muslim — an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, no. 2

The story of Jibril

Assalamu alaikum, I'm Heba. If this is your first time here: I'm summarizing Imam an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, one hadith at a time, using mind maps and simple explanations, for anyone who doesn't have time for long lessons. And remember: what cannot be done completely should not be left completely. So let's begin: hadith number two.

Imagine a calm gathering. The companions are sitting with the Prophet, peace be upon him. Suddenly, a man walks in. His clothes are extremely white, his hair is extremely black, there's no sign of travel on him, and none of them knows him. So who is this questioner?

بَيْنَمَا نَحْنُ جُلُوسٌ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه و سلم ذَاتَ يَوْمٍ، إذْ طَلَعَ عَلَيْنَا رَجُلٌ شَدِيدُ بَيَاضِ الثِّيَابِ، شَدِيدُ سَوَادِ الشَّعْرِ، لَا يُرَى عَلَيْهِ أَثَرُ السَّفَرِ، وَلَا يَعْرِفُهُ مِنَّا أَحَدٌ.

We were sitting with the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, one day, when a man appeared before us. His clothes were extremely white, and his hair was extremely black. No sign of travel could be seen on him, and none of us knew him.

The man sat close to the Prophet, peace be upon him, like a student who has come to learn. And he asked four questions: about Islam, then iman, which is faith, then ihsan, which is excellence, and then the Hour. At the end of the hadith, we find out he was Jibril, the angel Gabriel, who came to teach people their religion.

Islam & iman in context

Notice this: here, the hadith links Islam to the outward actions, and iman, or faith, to what's inside the heart. That's because the two were mentioned together, in the same context. But when one of them is mentioned alone, it includes the other. That's why scholars say: when they come together, they separate, and when they separate, they come together.

To make this rule clear, take two words: the poor, al-faqir, and the needy, al-miskin. The faqir has almost nothing. The miskin has a little, but not enough. If you say: help the faqir, on its own, the miskin is included too. But if you say: the faqir and the miskin, together, each word has its own meaning.

So what do outward and inward mean? Islam is what people see from you: your prayer, your fasting, your zakat, the obligatory charity, and your Hajj, the pilgrimage. And iman is what's inside your heart, which no one sees but Allah: that you believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and divine decree, al-qadar. And the two go together: the faith in your heart shows its effect in your actions.

Let's compare it to a tree. The roots are underground, and no one sees them, but they hold up the whole tree. That's like faith in the heart. The trunk, the branches and the fruit are above the ground, and everyone sees them. That's like Islam and our actions. A tree without roots won't stand, and roots without branches won't bear fruit.

The five pillars of Islam

الْإِسْلَامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لَا إلَهَ إلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ الْبَيْتَ إنْ اسْتَطَعْت إلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا.

Islam is that you testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, that you establish the prayer, give the zakat, fast Ramadan, and make the pilgrimage to the House if you are able to find a way to it.

So the pillars of Islam are five: the two testimonies of faith, called the shahada; establishing the prayer; giving zakat; fasting Ramadan; and Hajj to the House, for whoever is able. And the shahada is the foundation the whole building stands on.

The six pillars of faith

أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ بِاَللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ، وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالْقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ.

That you believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, and the Last Day, and that you believe in divine decree, both its good and its bad.

And the pillars of faith are six: belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and divine decree, both its good and its bad. These are matters of the unseen that we believe in with our hearts, and their effect shows in how we behave.

Decree & responsibility

Believing in divine decree doesn't mean we stop working. We believe that Allah knows everything and has written it, and that nothing happens except by His will. And yet, He gave us choice and free will, He commanded us to take the means, and we are responsible for our actions.

For example: a student studies well, then puts her trust in Allah and waits for the result, content with whatever comes. She took the means and accepted the decree. But someone who stops studying and says: it's already written, has misunderstood divine decree.

Ihsan: excellence

أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّك تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاك.

That you worship Allah as though you see Him, and if you do not see Him, then He surely sees you.

So ihsan, excellence, is the highest level of the religion. The building starts with Islam, then iman, then ihsan. And ihsan has two levels: that you worship Allah as if you see Him, and if you can't reach that level, remember that He sees you.

An example from everyday life: an employee does her report perfectly, even if no one will check it, because she knows Allah is watching her. And ihsan shows in your prayer, in your work, and in a kind word to people.

The Hour & the limits of knowledge

قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنْ السَّاعَةِ. قَالَ: مَا الْمَسْئُولُ عَنْهَا بِأَعْلَمَ مِنْ السَّائِلِ.

He said: Then tell me about the Hour. He said: The one being asked about it knows no more than the one asking.

Even the Prophet, peace be upon him, didn't give a date for the Hour, because its knowledge is with Allah alone. And this teaches us an important manner: to say, I don't know, about what we don't know, and to keep busy preparing for it, not with its date.

أَنْ تَلِدَ الْأَمَةُ رَبَّتَهَا، وَأَنْ تَرَى الْحُفَاةَ الْعُرَاةَ الْعَالَةَ رِعَاءَ الشَّاءِ يَتَطَاوَلُونَ فِي الْبُنْيَانِ.

That the slave woman gives birth to her mistress, and that you see the barefoot, the naked, the destitute, the shepherds of sheep, competing with one another in constructing tall buildings.

Review & practice

يَا عُمَرُ أَتَدْرِي مَنْ السَّائِلُ؟. قُلْتُ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: فَإِنَّهُ جِبْرِيلُ أَتَاكُمْ يُعَلِّمُكُمْ دِينَكُمْ

O Umar, do you know who the questioner was? I said: Allah and His Messenger know best. He said: That was Jibril. He came to you to teach you your religion.

Let's review together: Islam is outward actions, and it has five pillars. Iman is belief of the heart, and it has six pillars. Ihsan is to worship Allah as if you see Him. And the knowledge of the Hour is with Allah alone.

And to apply it this week: choose one act of worship and do it as well as you can. Review the pillars of Islam and the pillars of faith with your family. And ask yourself every day: am I working as if Allah sees me?
